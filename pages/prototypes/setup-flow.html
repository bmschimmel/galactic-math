SETUP FLOW

Three ways a kid could get from picking numbers to launching a game, now that Alien Invasion sits beside Galactic Math. Every prototype is clickable end to end — try each one, then read the notes under it and the comparison at the end.

A · Flight Deck

One big question per card. The kid only ever looks at one decision, taps it, and the next card bounces in. A flight path along the top shows where they are.

Numbers ▸ Operations ▸ Game ▸ Challenge ▸ Launch
Idea
Flashcard wizard. Single-choice steps (game, challenge) advance on their own after a short bounce, so most of the flow is tap-tap-tap with no "Next" button to find.
Taps to launch
5 for a returning kid who keeps the defaults (Next, Next, game, Standard, Launch). 6 with Hyperspace.
Strength
Lowest cognitive load — nothing on screen competes with the current question. Works identically on a phone and a desktop. Alien Invasion becomes a real choice instead of a footnote.
Watch out
More taps than today for a kid who only ever plays Galactic Math on defaults. Needs the flight path and Back to feel safe; the Mission Briefing card is what lets them fix a choice without starting over.
Build cost
Medium. Setup becomes five small screens; number/ops widgets move as-is, and game/mode state feeds the existing startQuiz() and launchAlienInvasion().

B · Mission Board

Ask the fun question first — "what do you want to play?" — then the challenge, and only then the math. Everything stays on one page: finished rows collapse to a summary line, the next row opens with a bounce, rows further down wait dimmed.

Game ▸ Challenge ▸ Numbers ▸ Operations ▸ Launch
Idea
Progressive disclosure on a single page. The kid always sees how many rows are left and can tap any finished row to change it. Numbers and operations come pre-set, so once a game and challenge are picked the launch bar lights up — tweaking the math is optional.
Taps to launch
3 with defaults (game, Standard, Launch). 4 with Hyperspace. Changing numbers or operations adds taps only for kids who want to.
Strength
Game-first matches how kids think ("I want the alien one"). Fewest taps of the three for the common case, and the launch bar's summary strip makes it obvious what is about to happen.
Watch out
Parents and teachers think math-first ("practice 7s and 8s"); the numbers row sits third. The page grows as rows open, so on a phone the launch bar should stick to the bottom of the screen.
Build cost
Medium-low. Same single setup screen as today, re-ordered and wrapped in collapsible rows; the launch button already exists.

C · Launch Bay

Closest to today. Numbers and operations stay at the top exactly as they are. Below them sit two equal ships. Tap a ship and it expands to hold its own challenge chips and its own launch button — nothing about a game lives outside its card.

Numbers ▸ Operations ▸ Ship (Game + Challenge + Launch)
Idea
Each game is a launch pad that owns its options. Replaces the dashed "Try Alien Invasion" link and the separate "Timed Challenge Modes" block with one consistent shape: pick a ship, set its challenge, hit its launch button.
Taps to launch
2 with defaults (ship, Launch). 4 with Hyperspace (ship, Hyperspace, difficulty, Launch).
Strength
Fewest taps; everything visible at once for grown-ups setting up a session; smallest change to the code and the muscle memory of existing players. Keeps the "numbers first" order the current app teaches.
Watch out
Busiest screen of the three — a 5-year-old sees numbers, operations and two ships before anything happens. The expanded ship pushes the page down, so the launch button may sit below the fold on a phone.
Build cost
Low. Restyles the existing setup card; mode tiles move inside the game card and the two launch buttons become one per game.

Reading the three

They pull in different directions on purpose. A optimises for the youngest players and phones at the cost of taps. C optimises for taps and for grown-ups, at the cost of a busy screen. B sits between them and is the only one that asks the "fun" question first.

Whichever wins, two things should carry over: Alien Invasion gets the same shape and weight as Galactic Math, and the challenge modes (Standard / Hyperspace / Kessel Run) are presented once, identically, for whichever game is chosen — the mode copy adapts to the game rather than the layout changing.
